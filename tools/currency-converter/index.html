The rate on this page, and the rate you get

The rates here are mid-market rates: the midpoint between what buyers and sellers are trading at. It’s the fairest reference there is, but almost nobody gets it when they change money. Banks, airport counters, exchange houses and transfer apps all quote a slightly worse rate and keep the difference. Many add a fee on top.

That margin is easy to miss because it isn’t written down as a fee. A counter can say “no commission” and still take 3% through the rate.

A worked example

Say the mid-market rate is 0.9100 euros per dollar and an airport counter offers 0.8800. On $1,000 you’d get €880 instead of €910. That’s €30 less, about 3.3%, even with no fee on the receipt.

Type the rate you’re offered in Check a rate you’ve been offered and the page works this out for your amount. Press Try an example to see it with today’s rates.

Getting a better rate

  • Compare the rate, not the fee. A provider with a small fee and a good rate often beats one with no fee and a poor rate.
  • Avoid airports and hotels when you can. Their rates are usually the worst.
  • Pay in the local currency when a card machine abroad asks. Letting it convert to your home currency usually costs more.
  • Check before big transfers. A 1% difference on a large amount is real money.
