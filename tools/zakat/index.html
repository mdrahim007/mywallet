What zakat is due on

Zakat is 2.5% of the wealth you’ve held for a full lunar year, once it’s above the nisab. Count cash at home and in the bank, mobile wallet balances, gold and silver, goods you hold to sell in a business, money you’re owed and expect to get back, and investments such as shares. Take away debts you have to pay now.

Your home, car, furniture and the things you use every day aren’t counted.

The nisab

The nisab is the least amount of wealth on which zakat is due. It’s set by weight: 87.48 grams (7.5 tola or vori) of gold, or 612.36 grams (52.5 tola or vori) of silver. Silver is far cheaper, so the silver nisab is much lower. Many scholars recommend it because more people give and more reaches those in need. Others use gold. Follow what your scholar or community follows, and type today’s local price, because metal prices change every day.

A worked example

Say silver is $1.05 a gram, which makes the silver nisab $642.98. You have $2,000 in cash, $8,500 in the bank, 20 grams of gold at $120 a gram, and a friend owes you $500. You have $1,200 of bills due now. Your net wealth is $12,200, well above the nisab, so zakat is $305. These prices are only for the example.

Where scholars differ

  • Jewellery you wear. Hanafi scholars count it; many others don’t.
  • Silver or gold nisab. Both are followed.
  • Long-term debts, pensions and investments. Views differ on what to count and what to take away.

This page does the arithmetic. It doesn’t give rulings, so ask a scholar you trust when you’re unsure.
