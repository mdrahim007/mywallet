How the instalment is worked out

Most bank loans charge interest on what you still owe, the reducing balance. Each month’s instalment is the same, but early on most of it is interest. As the balance falls, more of each payment goes to the loan itself.

Borrow $20,000 at 9% a year for five years and the instalment is $415.17 a month. Over the five years you pay $4,910.03 in interest, $24,910.03 in all.

Flat rate vs reducing balance

Some lenders quote a flat rate: interest on the full amount for the whole term, even after you’ve paid most of it back. It sounds cheaper than it is. A flat 9% on that same loan makes the instalment $483.33. That costs about the same as 15.71% on a reducing balance.

When two offers quote different kinds of rate, switch between Reducing balance and Flat rate above and compare the monthly instalment, not the headline number.

What paying a bit extra does

On a reducing-balance loan, every extra payment cuts the balance that interest is charged on. Adding $100 a month to the $20,000 loan clears it in 47 months instead of 60 and saves $1,183.03 in interest. Check first that your lender allows early payments without a fee.
