    The 50/30/20 rule

    It’s the simplest budget that works for most people. Of what you take home each month, about half goes on needs: rent, food, bills, transport and minimum loan payments. Up to 30% goes on wants, the things you could cut if you had to. At least 20% goes to savings, including paying debt off faster.

    It isn’t a law. In expensive cities rent alone can take a third of your pay, so 60/20/20 or 70/20/10 is more honest. Pick the split that fits, then use it to spot where the money really goes.

    A worked example

    Someone takes home $4,000 a month. Rent, groceries, bills, transport and a loan payment come to $2,500, which is $500 over the $2,000 that needs would get. Eating out, shopping and subscriptions come to $560, well under the $1,200 for wants. They save $400, half of the $800 target. And $540 isn’t planned for anything.

    The fix is clear once it’s on one screen: move $400 of the unplanned $540 into savings and the target is met. Then look at whether rent or the loan can come down. Press Try an example to see it.

    Making a budget that lasts

    • Use real numbers. Check last month’s bank statement instead of guessing. Guesses are usually too low.
    • Pay savings first. Move it on payday, not at the end of the month.
    • Leave room for fun. A budget with no wants in it doesn’t survive the first weekend.
    • Plan every unit of pay. Money that isn’t planned for anything tends to vanish.
