Turning a big number into a monthly one

A goal like “$12,000 for an emergency fund” is hard to act on. “$415.97 a month” isn’t. That’s what this page does: it splits the goal into the amount to put aside each month until your date, after counting what you’ve already saved.

If the money earns something, say in a savings account or deposit, add the yearly rate. Returns do part of the work, so the monthly amount comes down a little.

A worked example

You want $12,000 in two years and you’ve saved $1,500 already. With the money earning 4% a year, you need to put aside $415.97 a month. Over 24 months you put in $11,483.28, counting what you had, and returns add the last $516.72.

Switch to How long will it take? if you’d rather start from what you can afford. The page tells you the month you’ll get there.

Making it stick

  • Move the money on payday, before you spend anything. What’s left over at the end of the month is usually nothing.
  • Keep it somewhere separate. Money in your everyday account gets spent.
  • Start with an emergency fund. Without one, the first surprise bill comes out of your other goals.
  • Check in monthly. If you fall behind, adjust the amount or the date rather than giving up.
