      How the meal rate works

      In a shared mess, food costs are split by how much each person eats. Add up everything spent on groceries for the month and divide it by the total number of meals everyone ate. That’s the meal rate: what one meal cost.

      Each member’s food cost is their meals times the meal rate. Costs that don’t depend on eating, like the cook’s pay, gas, electricity and internet, are split equally. Compare each person’s total with what they paid in during the month, and you know who gets money back and who still owes.

      A worked example

      Six people share a mess. Groceries for the month come to $1,350 and they ate 300 meals between them, so the meal rate is $4.50. Shared costs are $450, or $75 each.

      Arjun ate 62 meals, so his share is $279 for food plus $75, which is $354. He paid in $400, so he gets $46 back. Imran ate only 30 meals: $135 plus $75 is $210, and he paid $200, so he owes $10. Everyone paid in $1,900 against $1,800 of costs, so the manager should have $100 left to hand back.

      Keeping the mess account clean

      • Count meals every day. A sheet on the fridge or a message in the group works. Month-end guessing doesn’t.
      • Keep every grocery receipt, or write the amount in the group chat as soon as the shopping is done.
      • Agree what “shared” means. Decide at the start which costs everyone splits equally.
      • Settle in the first week of the month. Post the copied summary in the group so everyone sees the same numbers.
