      Two ways to split a bill

      Split evenly when everyone ate about the same. Type the bill, add a tip if you like, and say how many of you there are. If you’re paying cash, round each share up to a round number. The page tells you how much extra that collects, so you can leave it as tip or hand it back.

      Split by what each person had when one person had a steak and another had soup. Add each item and tick who had it. A shared plate is divided between the people who shared it.

      Then type the total printed on the bill. The difference between that and the items is tax, VAT and service charge, and it’s shared by how much each person ordered. Someone who ordered twice as much pays twice as much of it. The tip works the same way, worked out on the food and drink before tax.

      A worked example

      Maya, Leo and Sam go out for dinner:

      • Pasta $18 for Maya, steak $32 for Leo, burger $16 for Sam
      • A $36 bottle of wine for Maya and Leo
      • A $15 starter platter for all three

      The items come to $117. With tax the bill says $128.70, and they add a 15% tip. Maya’s food and drink comes to $41, Leo’s to $55 and Sam’s to $21. So Maya pays $51.25, Leo $68.75 and Sam $26.25. That’s $146.25 in all.

      Leo put it on his card, so Maya sends him $51.25 and Sam sends him $26.25. Press Try an example at the top to see it.

      Why type the printed total?

      Restaurants add charges in different orders. Some charge tax on the service charge too, some include it in the prices, and some take a discount off. You don’t have to work any of that out. Type the last number on the bill and the page shares out whatever the difference is. If the bill is less than the items, the discount is shared out the same way.
