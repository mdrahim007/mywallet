      How the split is worked out

      Every expense is shared equally by the people ticked under Split between. Add up each person’s share across the trip, compare it with what they actually paid, and you have their balance. Paid more than their share, they get money back. Paid less, they owe.

      Then the person who owes the most pays the person who’s owed the most, and that repeats until everyone is even. So a group of five never needs more than four payments, and often fewer.

      A worked example

      Four friends spend a weekend at a lake:

      • Hotel, $480, paid by Maya, shared by all four
      • Car and fuel, $300, paid by Leo, all four
      • Groceries, $160, paid by Sam, all four
      • Kayak tour, $90, paid by Maya. Ana skipped it.

      Maya, Leo and Sam each come to $265; Ana’s share is $235. Maya paid $570, so she gets $305 back. Leo paid $300 and gets $35. Sam paid $160 and owes $105, and Ana owes her $235.

      Three payments settle it: Ana pays Maya $235, Sam pays Maya $70, and Sam pays Leo $35. Press Try an example at the top to see it worked out.

      Habits that keep trip money friendly

      • Agree before you leave what’s shared. The room and fuel usually are. Souvenirs and someone’s 2 a.m. pizza usually aren’t.
      • Write it down the same day. Receipts get lost, and by Sunday nobody remembers who paid for breakfast.
      • Keep one pot for the small stuff. Tea, tolls and tips are easier out of a shared fund than as twenty tiny expenses.
      • Settle within a day or two of getting home. After that it gets awkward to bring up.
